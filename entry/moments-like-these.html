Moments like these

There are moments that feel important while you're still in them. I've had a few. Most of the time, I only understood why later.

Oct 30, 2026 · 2 min read

The first one I remember clearly is my brother's high school graduation.

My then-girlfriend, now my wife, was there. Afterward I lay down in our old room and thought this was a turning point, not only for me but for my family. I knew the four of us, my grandma who helped raise me, my dad, my brother, and me, would never all be together in that house again. So I captured it like a Polaroid.

I didn't know exactly why at the time, but some part of me knew I'd look back someday, reflect, and understand why that moment stuck.

That habit has shown up more than once. Driving to the hospital just before my daughter was born, I saw a shooting star far off in the distance. I was also reading Zeldman's “Designing with Web Standards” in the cafeteria, eating a lopsided burger.

Some of them are smaller. Camping with my brother and Dad at a lake in California, the tent was wet, our eyes were groggy, and he set up the portable stove to make pancakes and bacon. I can still see that morning.

Others go back further. As a kid, I walked through the orange groves and picked one. Later, I spent nights at a friend's house watching David Lynch movies and messing around with stop-motion.

Work gave me a few more. I ran the rides at Castle Park, an amusement park in California, pushing the buttons that made them go. Later I cleaned schools, banks, and factories on a floor crew, which is the polite name for janitor. Before that, I was baling newspapers at 5:30 a.m., stripping wax off the floors around the press. Then I got married, and walked out to a car our friends had covered in "Just Married." They used paint that wouldn't wash off, so it stayed on the car for weeks, maybe months.

We capture them for reasons that make sense to us, even when we can't name those reasons at the time. Lately I'm doing it again. My kids are finding their own paths, and that's okay. Such is life.

Those are the moments I kept. Memory researchers say we rebuild a memory each time we recall it, so the picture I hold is probably not as full as I imagine it. It's still enough to bring me back.
